# AI use policy (template)

> **How to use this template.** Replace everything in [square brackets]. Then **delete every clause you are not prepared to follow.** A policy that promises more than you do is worse than a short one. Each clause shows the control it comes from and the kind of guarantee it can honestly be:
> **structural** (enforced in code), **procedural** (needs a named person), or **guided** (staff are asked to). Do not describe a guided clause as enforced.
>
> This is a starting point written for any non-profit. It is not legal advice. Have your own leadership approve it, and a qualified adviser review any clause that depends on a law or contract.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy covers every AI tool and AI feature that [Organization name] staff, volunteers, or contractors use, or that we build into our own services, including those used on personal devices for our work. [L7.02, L7.03]

We keep a register of the AI systems we use and what each is used for, with a risk rating. [L7.03 · procedural]

## 2. Level 1: Data protection (what the AI can see)

- We know which data each AI tool can reach, and we classify it as public, internal, confidential, or restricted. [L1.01 · procedural]
- Staff and volunteers must never type or paste the following into an AI tool: [list: for example credentials, payment details, case notes, health information, details of children]. [L1.06 · guided]
- AI tools act with the signed-in person's own access, never more. [L1.02 · structural]
- Restricted data is unreachable by AI unless a named person has deliberately opened it. [L1.03 · structural]
- Our privacy notice and consent practices cover AI use with personal data. [L1.08 · procedural]

## 3. Level 2: Vendor and model trust

- Before we adopt an AI tool we record what the vendor does with our data: training, retention, human review, and deletion. [L2.01 · procedural]
- Staff use AI through accounts the organization manages, not personal logins. [L2.02 · structural]
- We keep a list of which models and companies process our data, and where. [L2.03 · procedural]
- We have written terms covering confidentiality, data processing, breach notification, and deletion on exit. [L2.04 · procedural]
- Our commitment on constituent data: [for example, we do not sell it, we do not use it to train models, we do not pool it without consent]. [L2.06 · procedural]
- We re-run this assessment when a vendor changes its terms or a model is changed. [L2.08 · procedural]

## 4. Level 3: Delegation and authority (what the AI can do)

- Every action an AI can take is explicitly granted per role. Everything else is denied. [L3.01 · structural]
- Abilities to change records or send messages are off until a decision is recorded, and each grant has a named signer: [role]. [L3.02, L3.05 · procedural]
- The AI may draft or stage a change or message. A person commits or sends it unless a signed grant says otherwise. [L3.03 · structural]
- **Deletions, bulk messages, payments, and changes to protected records always need a person to commit them, whatever the grant.** [L3.04 · structural · human-dignity floor]
- Content from outside (uploads, inbound email, web pages) is treated as untrusted. [L3.07 · structural]
- There are hard limits on recipients per message, records changed per run, and spend per period. [L3.08 · structural]

## 5. Level 4: Protection of the vulnerable

- We have written down who counts as a protected person ([for example children, vulnerable adults, survivors, people in crisis]) and which records concern them. [L4.01 · procedural]
- Protected records are blocked or masked from AI unless a named person has opened them. [L4.02 · structural]
- **Photos and identifying details of children stay off AI paths, and we do not use AI to generate or edit images of identifiable children.** [L4.03 · structural · floor]
- **AI cannot send a message to a child without [a parent, guardian, or other required adult] included.** [L4.04 · structural · floor]
- Content about abuse, self-harm, or immediate danger is never sent automatically. It goes to [named role] and our reporting process. [L4.05 · procedural]
- **AI informs; people decide.** A named person decides eligibility for help, services, or care priority, and records the decision. [L4.07 · procedural · floor]
- **Any AI score, label, or classification of a person is visible to staff and can be explained.** [L4.09 · procedural · floor]
- **No AI output treats a person's worth or deservingness as depending on what they produce, achieve, or contribute.** [L4.12 · procedural · floor]
- We do not use AI to tailor appeals to exploit grief, crisis, age, or isolation. [L4.10 · procedural]

## 6. Level 5: Mission and values

- Our mission, commitments, and red lines are written down in [document and version] in a form an AI can be given and a person can audit. [L5.01 · procedural]
- That document was written and approved by [our governing body], not supplied by a vendor. [L5.04 · procedural]
- It is kept at a dated version, and changing it requires a recorded decision by [named role]. [L5.10 · procedural]
- AI-drafted content that states facts or speaks for us must cite sources and mark estimates. [L5.05 · procedural]
- Content sent in our name is reviewed by a person before release. [L5.08 · procedural]

## 7. Level 6: The human boundary

- AI never sends anything signed as a named person unless that person approved that specific message. [L6.01 · structural]
- **Public-facing AI says it is AI, and AI-drafted content is labeled where our policy or the law requires.** [L6.02 · structural · floor]
- **We do not offer member-facing AI that simulates care, friendship, or companionship. Care messages come from a named person.** [L6.03 · procedural · floor]
- Requests that need counseling, clinical, legal, or other professional judgment go to a qualified person. [L6.04 · procedural]
- Anyone using a public-facing AI can easily reach a person: [how]. [L6.08 · structural]

## 8. Level 7: Accountability

- The executive owner for AI is [name or role]. [L7.01 · procedural]
- **For each deployment we write down what the vendor, the software provider, and we each answer for.** [L7.04 · procedural · floor]
- We keep a log of AI activity that shows who used which tool, what happened, who granted the permission, and any human override. [L7.05, L7.06 · structural]
- An administrator can switch AI off immediately, for one user, one tool, or everything. [L7.07 · structural]
- We have an AI incident plan: [link to the incident response note]. [L7.08 · procedural]
- **A person affected by an AI-informed decision can ask for a human to review it, and staff can reconstruct how the decision was reached.** [L7.09 · procedural · floor]
- Before enabling write, send, or decision-support uses, we complete a short assessment of the impact on people, especially the most vulnerable. [L7.10 · procedural]
- Before we say anything to supporters, funders, or constituents about AI safety, we check that it matches what is in place. We do not describe a guided control as enforced. [L7.12 · procedural]
- We review this policy at least [every twelve months] and whenever something material changes. [L7.14 · procedural]
